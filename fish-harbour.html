The Sea and Its People

On the morning of 14 December 2025, I reached Vellayil Fish Harbour in Kozhikode at around 6:30 a.m. The harbour was already awake. Groups of fishermen sat together repairing fishing nets that had been torn during the previous trip at sea. It was careful, repetitive work, carried out almost without conversation, each person knowing exactly what to do. Along the harbour, boats stood side by side, their names painted across their bows—Devi Matha, Al Firdous, Mahabba, Musthafa—each carrying its own story and identity. The space was filled with sound. Engines rumbled as boats moved in and out of the harbour, ropes scraped against wood, waves struck the concrete edge, vendors called out to one another, fish crates were dragged across the ground, and gulls circled overhead, their cries cutting through the morning air. Nothing stood still.
